# Facts used in the film

| Claim on screen / in the voice | Value used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| GPS satellites fly about 20,200 km above the Earth | 20,200 km altitude (orbit radius ≈ 26,560 km) | GPS.gov, "Space Segment": "GPS satellites fly in medium Earth orbit (MEO) at an altitude of approximately 20,200 km" — https://www.gps.gov/systems/gps/space/ |
| Each satellite carries atomic clocks | rubidium / caesium clocks | GPS.gov, "Space Segment" |
| The receiver needs four satellites (x, y, z and its own clock error) | 4 | standard GPS trilateration; GPS.gov "How GPS works" |
| Signal travel time to you | 20,200 km ÷ 299,792 km/s ≈ 0.067 s | computed |
| Clocks in orbit run faster than on the ground by about 38 µs per day | +45.7 µs (gravity) − 7.2 µs (speed) ≈ +38 µs/day | N. Ashby, "Relativity in the Global Positioning System", Living Reviews in Relativity 6 (2003) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.12942/lrr-2003-1 |
| Uncorrected, the position error would grow by about 10 km a day | 38 µs × c ≈ 11.4 km/day ("about 10 km") | computed from the line above; widely quoted (e.g. Ohio State, "Real-World Relativity: The GPS Navigation System") |
| Smartphone GPS accuracy | "a few metres" (± 5 m drawn) | GPS.gov, "GPS Accuracy": GPS-enabled smartphones are typically accurate to within a 4.9 m radius under open sky — https://www.gps.gov/systems/gps/performance/accuracy/ |

Scale: the Earth's disc (6,371 km radius) and the GPS orbit (≈ 26,571 km radius) are drawn at one true scale in each frame; satellites, Pin and the location dot are markers, not to scale. The orbit is drawn flattened into one ring (the real constellation has six planes).
