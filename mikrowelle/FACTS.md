# Facts used in the film

| Claim | Value used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Household microwave ovens run at 2.45 GHz | 2.45 GHz | ITU ISM band 2.400–2.500 GHz; e.g. Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Microwave oven" |
| Wavelength 12.2 cm, so hot spots (antinodes of the standing wave) lie about 6 cm apart | λ = c / f = 12.24 cm, λ/2 = 6.12 cm | computed; the classic "measure the speed of light with chocolate in a microwave" experiment (e.g. Stauffer, *The Physics Teacher* 35, 231 (1997)) |
| The turntable moves food through the fixed hot and cold spots; the centre of the turntable does not move | geometry of rotation | standard explanation, e.g. USDA FSIS "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven" (rotate food, arrange in a ring) |
| Arranging food in a ring heats it more evenly | ring, hole in the middle | USDA FSIS "Cooking Safely in the Microwave Oven": "arrange food items evenly in a covered dish … in a ring" |

The heat image is a simulation, not a recording: a standing-wave heating pattern (two cavity modes, antinodes every λ/2), food starting at 6 °C (fridge), 120 s of heating, the rotation averaged over the turntable's turns (one turn in 10 s). Real ovens have more modes and conduction evens things out over time; the film shows the principle. Every number on screen is read from the same simulated sensor image that is coloured. The measured spot spacing (6.4 cm) differs slightly from λ/2 because the second mode shifts the peaks; the film labels it "≈ λ/2".
